The problem

Nobody has a safe answer to “what happens if I'm not here?”

Billions in crypto are lost forever when holders die. XRP's holder base skews long-term retail — people who bought years ago and hold in self-custody. It is exactly the population least likely to have an estate plan, and every option available to them today is bad.

Give someone your keys
They can take everything today, while you are alive and well.
Use a custodian
You stop self-custodying, which is the thing you were doing on purpose.
Write a legal will
The seed phrase ends up in a document handled by people you did not choose. Probate is public.
Do nothing
The coins are lost forever. This is what almost everyone does.

What is missing is a way to say “if I am gone, distribute my XRP like this” that nobody can act on early, nobody can read while you are alive, and nobody has to be trusted to carry out.

How it works

Four stages, and only one of them is irreversible

1

Seal FCC · HEIRLOOM/SEAL

Alice writes her will and it is encrypted to the enclave's key before it leaves her browser. Flare stores only a commitment. The enclave then decrypts it while she is alive and attests that it is readable — because a corrupt ciphertext discovered at execution is discovered too late to fix.

2

Live FDC · XRPPayment 0x08

She sends a periodic dust payment on the XRP Ledger to a shared beacon, carrying a destination tag unique to her vault. It costs a fraction of a cent and needs no Flare-side key. Anyone may relay the proof, so a holder who is alive but offline stays protected.

3

Silence FDC · XRPPaymentNonexistence 0x09

She stops. Anyone can now prove, cryptographically, that across an entire ledger range no tagged heartbeat reached the beacon. This opens a grace window. It releases nothing.

One late heartbeat — or Alice's own transaction — returns the vault to living and clears every guardian approval. Dormancy is a claim, not a verdict.

4

Distribute FCC · TEE-signed settlement

Only after the grace window elapses and guardians confirm does the enclave decrypt the will, price it against FTSO, and sign the XRPL payments. Flare verifies the signature and the commitment before recording the estate as settled. No custodian, no lawyer, no key handover — and nobody ever saw the will.

All the chain ever learns

A single hash over the will's canonical encoding. Change one beneficiary, one dollar, one basis point, and it changes — which is precisely what stops a substituted will from settling.

Why Flare

This product is not portable to another chain

Proving a payment happened is common. Proving that across an entire ledger range no matching payment exists is a fundamentally harder claim — and FDC is the only oracle that makes it verifiable on-chain.

Without XRPPaymentNonexistence there is no trustless dead-man's switch, only a keeper you have to trust to tell the truth about silence. Four enshrined protocols each carry weight nothing else provides:

Safety

The dangerous failure is not theft — it is a false positive

Distributing a living person's estate is worse than failing to distribute a dead one's, so every design decision leans that way. Guardians confirm; they never seize — they cannot read the will, alter a term, or move funds. The worst a fully colluding guardian set can do is fail to slow down a claim that was already cryptographically proven.

Three independent gates gate every settlement. The signature must recover to the registered TEE address, proving an attested enclave produced it. The revealed commitment must match what was sealed, proving it executed this will. And the price the enclave used must sit within 5% of the live FTSO feed, bounding how far a stale price could skew a fiat bequest.

When the estate cannot cover every fixed bequest, the bequests abate proportionally rather than being paid in list order until the money runs out. That is how estates actually settle — and the alternative silently rewrites the testator's intent based on an arbitrary ordering.

Limitations

Stated up front, because the alternative is a judge finding them

  • The TEE extension is not yet registered on live FCC infrastructure — that needs Docker, a public tunnel, and indexer-DB credentials from Flare. The logic is complete and tested, and the contract-side verification is byte-compatible with what TEE nodes actually sign: the tests produce real ECDSA signatures under that scheme and the contract accepts them.
  • The encrypted will currently rides in the instruction payload. The production path is an off-chain blob with only the commitment on-chain — which is already what settlement checks.
  • FXRP delivery is modelled and recorded end-to-end, but the transfer itself is not wired.
What's next

Inheritance is the demo, not the product

The primitive underneath is private, programmable rules governing native XRP, enforced by an enclave and triggered by provable on-ledger facts. The same machinery covers social recovery, vesting and savings locks, and corporate treasury continuity when a signer goes dark.

The allocation engine is deterministic precisely so independent enclaves can be cross-checked rather than trusted individually — getRandomTeeIds already supports fanning one instruction out to several.

Flare is working to bring more than two billion idle XRP into programmable finance. A meaningful share of it is idle precisely because its owners have no safe answer to what happens if I am not here. Heirloom is a reason for that XRP to touch Flare that has nothing to do with yield — which is what makes it durable in a way yield products are not.
